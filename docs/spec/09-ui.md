# 09 — UI

One React app (`apps/web`), components and exercise renderers included, for desktop and phone screens
(served by `sprachanker serve`; the desktop window: plan, "Desktop app"; phones: plan, "Phones").

## Layouts

- **Desktop (≥ 1024 px)**: sidebar navigation, main area; in the lesson a sidebar with the lesson plan,
  the tutor and reference tabs open in sheets. Keyboard shortcuts for answers and audio.
- **Phone**: bottom tab bar, lesson full screen, primary actions in the thumb zone, safe-area aware.
- Same routes and features on both; the layout adapts. Every profile is equal: no owner screens (06).
- Navigation: Courses · Review (with the number of cards due) · Notebook · Settings.
- Window title: "SprachAnker · <where you are>" in about ten characters (My courses, Course, Lesson 1.1,
  Ex 4/12, Summary, Settings); a running course download shows "↓ 45 %" there instead.

The tutor and scenes live in the lesson; there is no separate chat screen.

Planned (plan): an Exams section ("Exams"); synthesis settings ("Speech: synthesis"); the course
map's can-do list and a word tapped anywhere in a lesson opening its sheet (open items, "Review").

**Target language**: the open course's edition gives the language it teaches (`ru-en` → English); target-
language text carries that `lang`, answers are checked with its rules, and UI strings never name a language
(the writing task takes the language's name from `Intl.DisplayNames`).

**Writing direction**: courses explain in right-to-left languages too (Arabic, Persian): text blocks
take their direction from what they say (`unicode-bidi: plaintext`), and course HTML and model replies
their own (`dir="auto"`), so an Arabic instruction runs right to left and a German example in it left
to right; the interface itself stays in its language (en, ru, de).

**App icon**: on the yellow tile a dark speech bubble (up left) and a dark anchor (down right) overlapping it, kept apart by a gap in the tile colour so both read at 16 px (`apps/web/public/favicon.svg`,
the master); home-screen and install icons (iOS, PWA, maskable) are rendered from it, and the desktop app
takes its icons from it (desktop app, plan).

**Audio player**: play/pause, a timeline to drag or click (arrow keys jump 5 s), elapsed and remaining
time, speed 1× / 0.75× / 0.5× (a profile setting, shared by all audio); the full size (lesson audio,
exercise audio) adds a caption and "back 5 s", the compact one sits in an item. Single words keep a
one-tap button. One medium plays at a time; a file that cannot load says so.

A lesson without exercises (a vocabulary section) goes from the overview straight to the summary with its
words and the AI practice; the lesson tabs list only what the lesson has.

**Exercise page**: "Check" opens once every item is answered (a typed gap may stay empty) and every
recording of the exercise and its items has been played (one that cannot load blocks nothing); the
bar says what it waits for ("Answer every item · 1 of 2", "Play the recording of item 2"); "Skip" is
always there. After checking, each item's ✓/✕ sits on a rail left of its card, and its controls in a
column on the right with tooltips: "Why is this wrong?" (opens the explanation under the card) and
"Answer again" (that item only). Choices, gaps and pairs show the right option in place, order and
dictation add a "Correct answer:" line. Cards keep their size. The action bar is neutral, at the
bottom of the screen even under a short exercise, with fixed places: Back on the left, the main button
on the right with a fixed width ("Check" becomes "Next" under the finger).

**Matching**: one side gives places, the other chips put into them by dragging (mouse, finger,
keyboard) or by tapping a chip and then a place; a tap on a filled place gives its chip back. Pictures
are the places (each with a slot under it) and the words the chips; otherwise the left entries are rows
with a slot and the right ones the chips.

**Order**: a tap adds a part to the answer line, a tap in the line takes it back; a part can also be
dragged into the line, onto a part in it (it takes that place) or back to the pool.

**Word bank**: one gap is active (the first empty one, or the one tapped) and a tapped word goes there;
a word can also be dragged onto any gap; a tap on a filled gap takes its word back and makes it active.

Dragging is announced to screen readers in the interface language, naming the chip and the place.

**Order of options**: options, parts, word banks and drop-down lists are shown in an order of their own
for every pass of a lesson (seeded by the run), so a lesson done again is not answered by position;
within a pass — a reload, the Notebook showing it — the order stays (owner, 2026-10-07).

**Choices**: options that are all pictures are a grid of tiles; several choices show square boxes and
how many are chosen. Number keys 1–9 choose in the item to answer next (the one last touched, else the
first without an answer; one choice moves on to the next item); the options show no numbers, so nothing
changes size as the item to answer moves on (owner, 2026-10-07). Keys do nothing while typing or behind
an open panel.

**Typed answers** (gaps, dictation, free answers): a gap grows with what is typed (its width gives
nothing of the answer away); Enter goes to the next field of the exercise and from the last one checks
(Shift+Enter keeps a new line); under the fields a row of the language's letters a keyboard may lack
(German: ä ö ü ß; Shift for the capital, ẞ) types at the cursor.

**Saying it oneself** (speaking exercises, 08): the item's recording first (one that fails to load
does not block), then the microphone — a tap records, another stops — and one's take played beside the
original; the phrase to repeat is blurred until the first try or "Show the text" (Settings → Speech:
shown at once). Recorded is done. With recognition, under it what was heard and how it
went — "You said it", "Almost", "Try again" with the words missed (08); not part of the score. Items carry no numbers (owner, 2026-10-07); the rail shows an item's mark once checked. The
exercise's own picture, recording and texts sit in the items' column.

**Lesson plan** (one list, section labels: Grammar, Exercises, Culture, Practice with AI): every step
with its mark. Exercises: green ✓ all checked items right at the first attempt, red ✕ a mistake or a
checked item skipped, grey ✓ answered without a score (recorded, free answer), dashed ⏭ skipped
where nothing is checked, yellow ring the current one. Other steps: green-ringed ✓ done (a reading page
when opened, the words once "Done", AI practice once done), dashed ⏭ skipped, else their icon. The
summary lists every step with its mark and, in an open run, what is left with a way to the first one;
the lesson is completed when every step is done or skipped (05). A topic a grammar page continues from an earlier lesson says so
("· part 2"). The reference opens from the lesson's header; the plan lists the steps only.

**Words of the lesson**: opening the step makes the cards of the lesson's words, then Review's own
reviewer (`apps/web/src/study/`) runs over them (04): the same card widget, buttons, keys and actions
(a word already known is suspended or buried from the More menu). Once nothing is left to study now,
the step is done and says how many words go on learning in Review. A lesson card on the course map shows the run the lesson opens: "in progress · worked of all" or
"done · score".

**Course cards** (the Courses tabs): a band with the language pair as flags ("mine → the one
learnt", named on hover and for screen readers) and lessons done, a thin progress line, the title and
the source, the lesson to go on with ("Next: <title> · n of m done"), "Continue"/"Start" across the card
and "All lessons" under it. Long titles and next lessons are cut at two lines, the source at one
(whole on hover), so the buttons of a row line up. Flags are ours per language (`ui/flags.tsx`; English shows the British
flag, Arabic Saudi Arabia's, Swahili Tanzania's; every language a course is offered in has one); the course page and the language filter show them too.

**Downloading courses**: Courses → Available lists every course the providers offer. One not downloaded has a
dashed card with its source, licence and "Download · ≈ size"; while it downloads, cards and the course
page show a line with a bar — queued, pages read, lessons being prepared, media (files of all, MB) —
in place of "Start": a course can be started once it is fully downloaded. An incomplete one (files missing, or a download
that did not end) says so with "Continue downloading"; a complete one has "Sync with the source" on
its page. Settings → General → Storage shows the data folder, the space per source and the free space on the disk.

**Resetting progress**: "Remove from my courses" (the ✕ on a card in "My courses") and "Reset course
progress" (course page, next to sync) reset the course; "Reset lesson progress" on a lesson's overview
resets that lesson. Each asks first, saying what is deleted and that it cannot be undone (05).

Every screen has explicit empty and error states (no course installed, a course or lesson that is
not installed — a stale link — with the way to the available courses, no LLM configured, microphone unavailable
or on plain HTTP, the server not reached ("Try again"), a recording that cannot load), each with the action that fixes
it.

## First run

The first profile, and every new one, goes through the onboarding (Screens): its AI step sets up the
assistant (LM Studio included), its speaking step tests the microphone and downloads a recogniser
model; both optional.

## Design system

- **Character**: calm study tool; content first; large readable target-language text.
- **Colour**: grammatical gender is **reserved**: der = blue, die = red, das = green (plural neutral);
  these hues are used for nothing else. Accent: U-Bahn yellow. Correct/incorrect use other hues plus
  icons. Anki's colours take ours: Again and learning cards as wrong, Good and cards to review as
  correct, Easy and new cards a violet of their own (`srs-new`), Hard grey; only the seven flag icons
  keep Anki's colour names. Tokens as CSS variables; light and dark themes, following the system.
- **Type**: Barlow (UI, body), Barlow Semi Condensed (headings).
- **Components**: shadcn/ui on Radix + Tailwind; Lucide icons; Motion only where it informs; respects
  `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **Accessibility**: WCAG AA contrast, keyboard use on desktop (Space and Enter on a focused button or
  link are that control's, never a screen's shortcut), screen-reader labels (no link inside a
  button), touch targets ≥ 44 px on touch screens (`pointer-coarse:`, buttons by default).

## Internationalisation

- UI languages: English (default), Russian, German. All strings in ICU message
  catalogs; no hard-coded text; CI fails on missing keys. Dates, numbers, plurals via `Intl`.
- Content languages are independent of the UI language.
